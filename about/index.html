About

About four years ago, my life changed: ChatGPT arrived. At first I used it for writing emails and poetry. But as the models got smarter, so did what I could do with them: summarizing dense papers, explaining concepts I'd never quite grasped, turning "I don't get this" into "oh, that's actually simple." We were all warned it would hallucinate. It did. That never stopped me.

When the "thinking" models showed up, my software skills grew with them. I was writing automations constantly. Then Claude Code arrived and things clicked. I went from fiddling with clever prompts to shipping workflows that ran on their own.

Where that led

Today I'm the lead Forward Deployed Engineer on the AI Enablement team at WebMD. I embed with teams and map how they actually work before I touch any code.

My flagship project is FIM, the Feedback Intake Manager. I sat with the content editorial team to understand how they handled client feedback on marked-up proofs, then built a system that reads every comment, suggests which team needs to act, and flags what needs clarification. The goal was to change how a 150-person department across the US and UK handles client feedback. I built it alone in six weeks. I wrote the spec and the rules, AI coding agents did the typing, and nothing merged without passing the full test pipeline.

Building it taught me as much about hosting on legacy infrastructure and working around privacy constraints as it did about model design.

I've taken the same approach with other teams across WebMD, embedding with them to understand their workflows and build tools including the Sales Analyst Agent and the NBE Agent Fleet. The knowledge base and automations I built on my previous team helped lay the groundwork for that fleet.

Along the way, I also led WebMD's Perplexity Pro rollout and became a Perplexity Business Fellow. This past year, I ran AI Literacy Camp: demos and prompting workshops that reached 700+ people across the organization, alongside practical playbooks for Gemini and Google's AI tools. I bring the same instinct to local AI meetups in NYC, where I show up and sometimes speak: if something's working for me, I want it in other people's hands too.

How I work

I lead with implementing, shipping working systems into production, and treat teaching and rebuilding legacy processes as the supporting moves that make it actually stick. Abstract AI strategy that never ships doesn't interest me. What does: understanding the humans and systems already in place, then building the thing people will actually use.

Outside of WebMD, I build projects of my own, including Check Your Politician.
